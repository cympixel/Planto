Diffenbachia uses the standard C3 photosynthesis pathway, which is typical for most plants. In this process, carbon dioxide fixation and subsequent reactions occur simultaneously in the same cells during daylight hours. Diffenbachia’s large leaves provide a substantial stomatal surface area, boosting gas exchange and photosynthetic rate — but also increasing water loss.

Monstera also relies on C3 photosynthesis. Its large, perforated leaves increase light capture and improve air circulation, supporting efficient gas exchange. Monstera’s high humidity requirement is linked to C3 photosynthesis: in hot, dry conditions, the RuBisCO enzyme can “mistake” oxygen for CO₂, triggering photorespiration and reducing efficiency. Maintaining high humidity helps minimize this effect and keeps photosynthesis productive.

Banana palm tree follows the C3 photosynthesis pathway as well. With its extensive leaf surface, it’s capable of intense photosynthesis when provided with sufficient light and moisture. However, in typical indoor settings, the plant often lacks enough light and warmth, so its actual photosynthetic rate tends to be lower than its potential. To maximize productivity, it needs near‑tropical conditions: abundant light, warmth, and consistently moist soil.
